# Garble patterns: more examples of the eight shapes

_Part of Torah Transcript Scribe, a free Claude plugin from [PowerJews.com](https://www.powerjews.com)._

These are illustrations of mechanisms, gathered from several speakers and engines, not a lookup table. The same word garbles differently in every mouth and every engine, so when a suspect resists, ask which shape it is and re-hear it, rather than searching this file for the exact string.

Format: canonical form ← typical garbles (notes).

## Shape 3. Phrase soup (quoted Hebrew and Aramaic)

A quoted line comes out as pseudo-words. The speaker's translation, usually in the next clause, is the key. Reconstruct the real line, then check it against the source when you can.

## Shape 7. Deletions and stubs

The engine drops a Hebrew word it cannot place and the sentence closes over the hole: "the were the ones who", "according to the on this verse". Sometimes only the first sound survives as a stub: "in v—" (Vayikra), "from the l—" (lions), "the m—" (Midrash), "not in the but in Tan" (Tanakh). A stub is a strong clue: list the Hebrew words that begin with that sound and fit the sentence. Restore only when the sentence or the cited content demands exactly one candidate (the term named two sentences earlier, a name the speaker is in the middle of discussing, the sefer where the verse he is paraphrasing actually sits). Otherwise leave the hole or stub and flag it. YouTube captions in particular delete Hebrew nouns outright far more often than they garble them, so in a caption transcript expect holes after "the", "my", "your", "what is the", and after "from" and "called" where a sefer name belongs.

## Shape 8. English-to-English swaps

The engine chose the more frequent English word. Context, not sound, reveals these, so read narrative passages for sense, including audience lines.

- raped ← ripped (Dinah, Tamar, Pilegesh b'Givah)
- killed ← filled; bribe ← bride; robbed ← rubbed
- traitors ← traders (kings context)
- kabbalistic ← ballistic
- haughty ← hotti
- reassigned ← rationed; palace ← pal
- phys-ed ← fizz; harpsichord ← harps accord
- Kyiv Post ← kav of post (modern media names garble too)

## Confusable pairs: do not guess

- Tehillim (Psalms) vs tefillin (phylacteries)
- Mishnah vs mishpat vs Mishnah Berurah
- Halacha vs Haggadah vs aggadah
- sefer (book) vs sofer (scribe)
- Rav vs Rava vs Rabbah vs Rebbi (Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi) vs Rabbi Abba
- Rabbi Elazar vs Rabbi Eliezer; Rav Huna vs Rav Hamnuna; Rav Yosef vs Rabbi Yose vs Rav Assi
- Rema (Rav Moshe Isserles) vs Rama MiFano vs Ramak vs Ramchal vs Rambam vs Ramban
- Rashi vs Rashba vs Rashbi vs Rashbam
- Chanoch (Enoch) vs Chanukah; Shabtai (Saturn, or Shabtai Tzvi) vs Shabbat
- Aharon vs Haran vs Charan
- Shechem (the person) vs Shechem (the city); Chamor (Shechem's father) vs chamor (donkey)
- bar mitzvah vs bas/bat mitzvah: gender from context
- Yaakov vs Yerovam when the garble is "yov": northern-kingdom context decides

## What not to fix

- Shabbos / Shabbat, beracha / bracha / berakha, hilchos / hilchot, chasidus / hasidut, yeshiva / yeshivah, Moshiach / Mashiach, Sh'ma / Shema: regional style, preserve.
- Code-switching mid-sentence between Hebrew and English: keep as spoken.
- A speaker's deliberately loose terminology for teaching ("the Sumerian word" for a Ugaritic one): keep, note in the review if it matters.
- A speaker's own arithmetic in a gematria that does not add up: keep as spoken, log the correct value in the review.
- Yiddishisms and frum-English ("nu", "takeh", "mamash", "davka", "shtark", "geshmak", "oilam", "chevra"): preserve, spelled consistently.
- "Rabbi" versus "Rav" versus "Reb": the speaker's choice.

## Meta-rule

When a word looks wrong and you cannot pin the right form: check the surrounding context (topic, which sefer, who is quoted), re-hear it through the eight shapes, verify any candidate against what exists. If still uncertain, leave the original word and flag it. Never invent a Torah source or a rav name to fill a gap.
